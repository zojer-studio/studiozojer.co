This update is about clearing the table. We've been listening to feedback, and the main thing we're hearing is that Kairōs can feel overwhelming — too many tools on screen, too many things competing for your attention. So we're pulling back and making space.

### What changed

- **Chart tools collapse** — the chart controls now hide behind an expand/collapse button. The full power is still there when you need it; it just isn't staring at you when you don't.
- **Selection pills** are easier to read on the chart view.
- **Ingresses and stations** are now visible in the ephemeris feed (today page) on request. (Thanks for the feedback!)
- **Today page rows** now open the relevant chart directly. You can still access the codex entry by selecting the planet(s) in the chart.
- **Codex streamlined** — recent entries are tucked under a card. The codex is still fully there, but it's no longer the first thing you see.
- **Retrograde codex entries** added to the default library.
- **Selection dimming** — added more global controls into the display profile, to make your charts "pop" a bit more when selected.

# Where we're headed

In the last set of release notes, we talked about the codex as the heart of the app — a shared dictionary, a social space for astrologers to talk about transits together. That vision isn't going away, but we're rethinking the order of operations.

What we've realized is that before Kairōs can be a great social tool, it needs to be a great *personal* tool. You should be able to open the app and immediately understand what's happening in the sky and what it means for you — without needing to build a library of entries first.

So we're shifting our focus toward the **Today page**. Over the coming updates, it's going to become significantly more useful — a place where you can see the transits that matter to your chart, understand their cycles, and track how they unfold over time. Think of it less as a feed and more as a living horoscope, built on real astrology.

The codex, daily notes, and social features will continue to grow alongside this — but the foundation we're building now is about making the planets legible to you, personally, the moment you open the app.

More to come. Thanks for being here while we figure it out.
